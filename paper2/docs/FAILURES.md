Each failure mode is grounded in exact empirical evidence and raw data files from Paper 1.

## 2. Quantitative Problem Statement for Paper 2

> **Problem Statement:**  
> Contemporary high-dimensional clustering methods fail in two critical real-world regimes: (i) datasets with non-linear multi-feature interactions or zero-inflated sparse counts, where marginal 1D feature screening is blinded by unimodal marginals or distorted by zero-mass spikes; and (ii) complex multi-class structures ($K \ge 16$), where uniform subspace sampling yields high seed variance (bimodal failure) and spectral coordinate blending. Practitioners currently lack an unsupervised clustering framework that can simultaneously uncover non-linear multi-attribute dependencies, self-calibrate feature saliency against permutation null models, dynamically estimate cluster count $K$, and project unseen out-of-sample instances with finite-sample coverage guarantees without suffering from the consensus stability-accuracy trap.
