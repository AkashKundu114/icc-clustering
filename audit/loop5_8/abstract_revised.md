> We examined the relationship between resampling stability and external reference-label alignment in six biomedical clustering cohorts. For each cohort and method, stability was summarized as the mean adjusted Rand index across pairs of partitions on overlapping samples from 30 label-stratified 80% subsamples; external alignment was summarized as the mean adjusted Rand index against the reference labels across those subsamples. These summaries measure different properties. Across 72 cohort–method cells, mean stability was 0.517 and mean external ARI was 0.052; the mean cell-level difference was 0.464 (median 0.448). The calculation is descriptive: cells share cohorts and methods, and pairwise stability values reuse the same partitions. The resampling scheme preserves reference-label proportions and therefore is not a fully label-blind sampling design. A post-hoc non-degeneracy analysis identified a smaller subset with high stability and low external ARI; we treat that subset as exploratory. Separately, saved external-ARI scores from a 13-dataset benchmark were recalculated as a distinct comparison and do not extend the stability analysis to those datasets. These results show that, under the examined protocol and in these cohorts, repeatability and reference-label alignment need not coincide; they do not establish a universal relationship or clinical validity.
