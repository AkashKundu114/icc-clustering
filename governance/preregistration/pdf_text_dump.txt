Abstract
Consensus clustering synthesizes base partitions to discover robust data group-
ings. However, existing methods face two primary challenges: (1) a transductive
formulation requiring O(N 2) storage preventing out-of-sample inference, and
(2) vulnerability to high-dimensional distractor noise (D ≫N), where distance
concentration degrades partition fidelity. We examine Inductive Consensus
Clustering (ICC-v2), designed for small-sample, high-dimensional regimes
subject to isotropic noise. ICC-v2 incorporates: (1) an O(N · D) linear feature
screening filter using Sarle’s Bimodality Coeﬀicient (Bimodality-Gated Subspace
Selection, BGSS) isolating multimodal dimensions; (2) a determinacy-based
bipartite anchor consensus graph reducing auxiliary memory from O(N 2) to
O(N · L); and (3) stability-weighted centroid assignment for O(K · |Factive|)
inductive inference. Across 13 multi-domain benchmarks (1,430 model fits), deep
embedded clustering (DEC) attains the lowest omnibus mean rank (R = 4.31),
followed by Standard Consensus (R = 5.08), K-Means (R = 5.31), Ward
(R = 5.31), and ICC-v2 (R = 5.46). ICC-v2 attains the highest Grand Mean

1 Introduction
Clustering ensembles, originally pioneered by Strehl and Ghosh [1] and Fred and
Jain [2], aim to integrate multiple base clusterings into a singular consensus par-
tition. By aggregating diverse hypothesis partitions generated through randomized
initializations, varied clustering algorithms, or random feature projections, consen-
sus clustering mitigates the sensitivity of individual algorithms to hyperparameter
selection and initialization bias [3? , 4].
Despite wide adoption across bioinformatics, computer vision, and knowledge
discovery, consensus clustering algorithms encounter two primary limitations:
1. Transductive
Formulation:
Conventional
consensus
formulations
operate
directly on the N × N co-association matrix S ∈[0, 1]N×N via graph partition-
ing, spectral decomposition [5], or non-negative matrix factorization [6]. When
new, unseen test instances Xtest arrive, the consensus partition cannot be induc-
tively assigned without recomputing the co-association matrix and solving an
O((N + |Xtest|)2) optimization from scratch.
2. Curse of High-Dimensional Distractors: In modern datasets where ambi-
ent dimensionality D greatly exceeds the intrinsic manifold dimension d∗≪
D, uninformative nuisance features degrade pairwise Euclidean distances. The
distance concentration phenomenon causes within-cluster and between-cluster dis-
tances to converge to identical expectations, leading to degradation of consensus
graphs [7–9].
To address these limitations, this paper investigates the Inductive Consen-
sus Clustering (ICC) framework (Fig. 1). We examine the finite-sample empirical
breakdown of consensus stability under isotropic distractor noise (Section 3), illus-
trating how distance concentration generates consensus agreement on arbitrary
noise boundaries. Motivated by these findings, we formulate ICC-v2, a four-stage
framework:

2.2 Robust, Anchor, and Out-of-Sample Clustering
To mitigate noise and outliers, Li et al. [6] proposed determinacy core spectral ensem-
ble clustering, partitioning samples into deterministic core points and boundary points
based on co-association variance. In scalable spectral and ensemble clustering, anchor
graph methods (e.g., Nie et al. [11], Huang et al. [12], and U-SPEC/U-SENC by Huang
et al. [? ]) utilize bipartite graphs to reduce spectral decomposition complexity from
O(N 3) to O(NL2 + L3). For out-of-sample extension, generalized Nyström formu-
las [? ] project unseen points using kernel evaluations. In high-dimensional regimes,
sparse clustering methods (e.g., sparse k-means by Witten and Tibshirani [? ]) opti-
mize lasso-type penalties on feature weights, but require iterative global optimization.
Similarly, non-parametric multimodality testing (e.g., the Dip Test by Hartigan and
Hartigan [? ] and Sarle’s Bimodality Coeﬀicient [10? ]) provides statistical screening
criteria. As summarized in Table 1, ICC-v2 combines linear bimodality subspace gat-
ing, determinacy-based bipartite anchor consensus, and inductive centroid projection
into a unified linear-time pipeline.

Computing ˆbd requires only empirical second, third, and fourth moments across
instances, executing in strictly O(N · D) time. We state the underlying assumption
and failure modes of VSSG:
Assumption 1 (Marginal Multimodality Prior and Failure Modes) Informative subspace
dimensions separating ground-truth clusters exhibit marginal multimodality (ˆbd ≥5/9),
whereas nuisance distractor dimensions are continuous and unimodal (ˆbd ≤5/9).
Remark 1 (Failure Modes of Marginal Screening) Assumption 1 encounters boundary
conditions in the following settings:
1. Skewed Unimodal Features: Unimodal distributions with substantial skewness
(e.g., lognormal distributions or skewed count data) can yield ˆbd ≥5/9 in the
absence of true clustering.
2. Non-Linear Parity Interactions: Cluster structures governed by multi-feature
parity or XOR interactions (exemplified by the Madelon benchmark, where D =
500 with 5 informative, 15 redundant, and 480 distractor dimensions) exhibit Gaus-
sian 1D marginals with ˆbd ≤0.504 < 5/9, preventing detection via independent
1D projections.
3. Sparsity and Zero-Inflation: Under highly sparse representations (e.g., scRNA-
seq counts with ≥90% zeros), the point mass at zero inflates skewness and kurtosis.
In the implementation, rather than applying a strict analytical cutoff ˆbd ≥5/9
(which may produce an empty set on parity-like benchmarks), the default pipeline
sorts features by ˆbd and selects the top candidate pool of size min(D, max(K, ⌊α·D⌋))
with default α = 0.15 and minimum cap min(100, D). On Leukemia (D = 7,129), an
unconstrained threshold ˆbd ≥5/9 isolates 1,522 genes, whereas the default capped
selection rule retains ⌊0.15 × 7,129⌋= 1,069 candidate genes.

4.4 Anchor-Space Perturbation Profiling (ASPP)

5.7 Limitations
We document several explicit limitations of the proposed framework:
1. Large Cluster Counts (K ≥20): With a fixed anchor budget of L = 40,
datasets containing K ≥20 clusters (e.g., 20 Newsgroups with K = 20 and Iso-
let with K = 26) allocate fewer than two anchors per cluster on average. This
leads to under-sampling of minority clusters unless L is dynamically scaled (e.g.,
L = max(40, 10K)) with stratified sampling.
2. Sparse,
Zero-Inflated,
or
Skewed
Features: VSSG relies on marginal
moments. Features with heavy point masses at zero (e.g., single-cell RNA counts or
TF-IDF text features) or high skewness can artificially inflate Sarle’s Bimodality
Coeﬀicient, selecting uninformative dimensions.
3. Non-Linear Parity Interactions: As demonstrated on Madelon, marginal 1D
screening cannot detect feature interactions without marginal separation.
4. Known Cluster Count K: ICC-v2 assumes the number of clusters K is provided
a priori and does not perform automated model order selection.
5. Small Sample Size Variance: For small sample sizes (N < 30), sample third and
fourth moments have high statistical variance, which can impair feature ranking.
6. Baseline Comparison Scope: In the primary benchmark (Table 3), competing
baselines were evaluated on the full ambient feature set without dedicated upstream
feature selection.
7. Sample Requirements for Deep Clustering: Deep clustering (DEC) under-
performs on small sample regimes (N ≤100) due to insuﬀicient training data for
autoencoder representations.
8. Near-Zero ARI Regimes: On CNS, Colon, Prostate, and Ovarian, all evaluated
methods achieve low ARI (≤0.052), indicating unresolved clustering challenges on
these benchmarks.

6 Conclusion
This paper investigated Inductive Consensus Clustering (ICC), addressing the trans-
ductive formulation and noise vulnerability of classical cluster ensembles. Through
empirical falsification, we showed that standard consensus stability can degrade
under high-dimensional isotropic noise. The proposed ICC-v2 framework integrates
Variance-Stabilized Subspace Gating (VSSG), determinacy-based bipartite anchor
consensus, and stability-weighted centroid projection. Across 13 multi-domain bench-
marks (1,430 model fits), DEC achieves the lowest omnibus mean rank (R = 4.31),
with ICC-v2 ranking fifth (R = 5.46). ICC-v2 achieves the highest Grand Mean
ARI of 0.160 and attains mean rank R = 2.33 on the exploratory high-dimensional
biomedical subgroup (D/N > 20, with an absolute ARI improvement of +0.498
over Standard Consensus on SRBCT), while reducing auxiliary consensus memory by
97.0% at N = 10,000 and executing up to N = 1,000,000 instances in 55.6s. Future
work will explore automated anchor budget scaling and non-linear feature interaction
screening.
